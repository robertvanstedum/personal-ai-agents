# Mini-moi Work

## Robert's intent for an accumulating partnership

**Status:** Controlling product direction; living document  
**Decision owner:** Robert van Stedum  
**Initial articulation:** September 4, 2026  
**Scope:** Chief of Staff, collaborative Work, Career as the first proving ground

## The vision

Mini-moi should become an enduring, person-owned system through which Chief of
Staff grows into a real working partner for Robert: someone who knows him,
understands the history behind the present question, helps him make better
judgments, creates useful work with him, and carries what was learned into the
next piece of work.

It should not merely retrieve facts about Robert or produce fluent text that
sounds vaguely like him. It should develop continuity: what Robert has done,
how he thinks, what he values, where his strongest evidence lies, what he has
already rejected, what he is trying to become, and how those things should
change the advice and work produced now.

Chief of Staff is the relational center of that experience. Voice and chat are
the natural front door. The technology behind the conversation should make the
relationship more capable and cumulative without turning Robert into the
operator of a database, a workflow application, or a collection of forms.

Career cover-letter work is the first serious expression of this vision. It is
immediately useful in Robert's job search, and it is a demanding prototype of
the larger Mini-moi idea. If Chief of Staff can understand an opportunity,
select truthful evidence from Robert's life, challenge weak positioning,
collaborate through repeated revisions, preserve Robert's own language, and do
better the next time, then Mini-moi has begun to demonstrate something much
more important than letter generation.

It has begun to demonstrate understanding that accumulates.

## Why Career is the right first proving ground

A cover letter is deceptively difficult. It sits at the intersection of fact,
judgment, voice, aspiration, privacy, and action.

Chief of Staff needs to know which parts of Robert's history matter for a
particular opportunity and which true accomplishments are nevertheless the
wrong evidence. It needs to understand the difference between what a company
says it wants and what it likely needs proven. It needs to connect technical,
commercial, operational, and human experience without overstating any of it.
It needs to help Robert sound like himself rather than like a generated
candidate. And it needs to recognize that sometimes the best advice is not to
apply.

That makes the work practical now and foundational later. The same underlying
ability will be useful for interview preparation, a decision memo, a project
brief, a meeting narrative, a presentation, or professional work after Robert
lands a role. The nouns and source material will change. The deeper motion—
understand, assemble context, reason, collaborate, preserve, and resume—will
not.

Career is therefore a subject area within the Chief of Staff relationship, not
a separate Mini-moi product and not the vocabulary of the common foundation.

## The relationship comes first

Chief of Staff is Robert's partner, confidant, and trusted advisor. Its purpose
is not to complete the largest number of tasks. Its purpose is to help Robert
think, decide, create, and follow through with greater continuity and less loss
of hard-won context.

That relationship should be candid and intelligent. Chief of Staff should ask
questions when Robert's intent is unclear, disagree when the evidence points
elsewhere, notice tensions with longer-term goals, and surface relevant things
Robert did not remember to request. It should not flatter him by reflecting his
language back at him or confuse familiarity with agreement.

Across Mini-moi, Chief of Staff may consult the other domains and ask for their
updates. It may connect what they reveal and help Robert understand the whole.
It does not act inside those domains or become their supervisor. Domain
ownership and authority remain intact.

Within personal subject areas such as Career, Chief of Staff can have bounded
skills of its own. It can help work with resumes, cover letters, application
responses, opportunity decisions, and eventually interview preparation. These
skills deepen the partnership; they do not turn Chief of Staff into an
autonomous applicant or external representative.

## A place where conversation can become work

Most conversation should remain conversation. Robert should be able to think
aloud, ask a question, change direction, or reflect without every exchange
becoming a tracked object.

Sometimes, however, a conversation becomes substantive work. A source is
introduced. A draft begins. Robert wants to leave and continue tomorrow. A
decision or artifact becomes worth keeping. At that point the conversation can
acquire a lightweight, durable Work context.

The visible experience should still feel like Robert and Chief of Staff at
work together. A Work area may show the current source, the latest artifact,
and what was decided, but it should remain centered on voice, chat, and direct
editing. Robert should not have to create records, select lifecycle states,
maintain metadata, or understand storage paths.

The machinery may be exact underneath because truth, privacy, recovery, and
authority require it. The experience above that machinery should stay natural.

## The first Career experience

Robert brings Chief of Staff a job posting or begins discussing an opportunity.
The first question is not automatically “write the letter.” They work out
whether the opportunity is interesting, what kind of fit it represents, what
the company actually needs demonstrated, and where the weak or uncertain parts
of the case lie.

Chief of Staff draws from the sources Robert has authorized: the current resume,
his prior writing, past application responses, relevant stories and experience,
and what their relationship already knows. It distinguishes Robert's words and
verified history from outside material, earlier agent drafts, and its own
inferences.

It then helps form the argument. A useful lightweight discipline is three or
four questions rather than a form:

1. What does this company or role need proven?
2. Which few parts of Robert's history prove it truthfully?
3. Why does that evidence matter commercially or operationally here?
4. What is the personal insight or language that only Robert would add?

These are working prompts, not required database fields. Their purpose is to
focus thought and keep the letter specific.

Chief of Staff drafts and challenges. Robert responds, rewrites, or takes the
keyboard. His direct participation is not a usability failure. For an important
opportunity it is part of the desired relationship: the letter becomes genuinely
co-authored, and the work reveals more of Robert than a resume ever could.

They may stop and resume without losing the posting, the latest draft, the
important sources, or the point of the work. When Robert approves the text, the
approved artifact can be retained. Robert—not Chief of Staff—submits it or
communicates externally.

## Two legitimate modes of collaboration

### Important opportunities

When Robert cares about a job, the work should be exploratory and hands-on.
Chief of Staff helps him see the opportunity, questions the positioning,
retrieves possible evidence, and creates something worth reacting to. Robert
expects to add language, change emphasis, and sometimes rewrite substantially.

The first important letter may take ten passes. That is not evidence that the
idea failed. It is the first deep calibration session: the place where Robert
and Chief of Staff discover which parts of their shared understanding are
missing, wrong, too generic, or not yet expressible in his voice.

### Exploratory opportunities

For a role Robert is testing rather than pursuing deeply, the mature capability
should adapt trusted material with much less effort. Chief of Staff still checks
fit and uses truthful, role-specific evidence, but Robert may choose a standard
letter, review the adaptation, make a few changes, and submit it himself.

Exploratory does not mean careless, generic, or automated. It means the amount
of collaboration is proportionate to Robert's current interest.

The modes can change. An exploratory role may become important after a
conversation. An important role may lose its appeal. The relationship should
follow Robert's judgment rather than freeze a lane at intake.

In either mode, `do not apply` is a useful result when the fit, opportunity, or
tradeoff is poor.

## The 80/20 idea: accumulated understanding, not a template

Robert's practical aspiration is that much of the hard work should become
reusable. Roughly 80 percent is the understanding that should carry: his real
evidence, positioning, voice, recurring story lines, claims he is comfortable
making, and the shape of the case for a family of roles. Roughly 20 percent is
new: what this company needs, why this role matters, which evidence belongs
here, and the insight or language that makes this application personal.

The ratio is a way to think, not a metric Robert must track. The point is
compounding. Letter two should not restart from zero. It should benefit from
letter one and add the next increment. This is the n+1 idea: each serious
collaboration should make the next related one better and cheaper without
making it less personal.

The reusable understanding will not be identical across every job. Natural
families will emerge—for example, business-and-technology product or presales
roles, senior leadership roles, startup roles, and communications-domain roles.
A new family may require another substantial calibration. These families are
useful working distinctions, not a taxonomy Robert should have to design or
maintain in advance.

The 80 percent must not collapse into generic prose. A stable template that
could belong to anyone is not understanding. The reusable layer must remain
specifically Robert's: his evidence, his way of connecting business and
technology, his judgments about why work matters, his lived stories, and his
preferred degree of directness.

The system is working when Robert is mostly changing the opportunity-specific
20 percent or adding something genuinely new. If he repeatedly repairs the
same underlying evidence choice, positioning, or voice, the shared understanding
has not yet accumulated.

Again, Robert does not need a dashboard to prove this. He is the client. He
will feel whether the collaboration is carrying forward or starting over.

## A corpus Robert will actually use

The source material should fit Robert's natural behavior. He does not want a
structured CRUD system for every story, answer, or preference. He wants to be
able to put his material in a private place—including a loose “Other responses”
folder—and have Chief of Staff search and reason over it.

Past answers matter because they preserve what Robert thought, what he chose to
say, and how he approached a similar question. They are not paste-ready stock
answers. A “Why this company?” response should still be newly reasoned for the
company in front of him. The earlier material supplies ingredients, language,
and remembered thought—not recycled sincerity.

The most important distinction is authorship. A folder that represents
Robert's words must not quietly fill with unreviewed agent prose. Otherwise
Chief of Staff will later retrieve its own writing as evidence of Robert's voice
and create a closed imitation loop. Agent drafts can remain work artifacts.
Robert-authored or explicitly approved/co-authored material can become part of
the corpus from which future work benefits.

The system should derive useful structure at read time where an agent can do so
reliably. It should impose structure only when needed to protect provenance,
privacy, authority, or recovery. A folder Robert actually uses is more valuable
than a perfect schema he avoids.

## What it means to learn Robert

The objective is not to build a statistical caricature of Robert or accumulate
every edit as a permanent preference. Nor is it to make Chief of Staff sound
more like Robert while continuing to choose the wrong evidence.

Useful understanding has several dimensions:

- factual grounding in Robert's real history;
- judgment about which facts matter in the situation;
- positioning that connects his experience to the other party's needs;
- memory of prior decisions and rejected framings;
- sensitivity to his voice without impersonating him;
- awareness of longer-term goals and tensions; and
- the ability to make a connection or suggestion that Robert recognizes as
  useful even though he did not explicitly supply it in that turn.

Chief of Staff should learn from Robert's sources, his explicit corrections,
his approved work, and the reasons discussed during collaboration. It should
not learn Robert from its own unreviewed drafts. It should not convert every
one-off edit into identity memory. When a possible durable lesson is ambiguous,
it can raise it conversationally and let Robert decide whether it should carry.

Four epistemic distinctions must remain intact even when the experience feels
fluid:

1. what Robert just said;
2. what Robert previously wrote or approved;
3. what the system retrieved from another source; and
4. what the agent inferred or proposed.

That honesty is part of the relationship. Familiarity without provenance would
make Chief of Staff more persuasive and less trustworthy.

## Person-owned and product-independent by design

Mini-moi is not built to Grok, OpenClaw, Claude, Codex, or any other product.
There must be no tight coupling or required dependency on one model, runtime,
host, interface, or vendor.

The durable capability belongs to Robert and Mini-moi:

- Robert's sources and work artifacts remain person-owned, private, readable,
  and portable;
- Mini-moi owns the common behavior, authority boundaries, and continuity;
- canonical memory and work survive a model, runtime, or interface change;
- products can provide reasoning, tools, channels, or execution through thin,
  replaceable boundaries; and
- removing a product must not require reconstructing Robert's history or
  migrating the meaning of his work.

OpenClaw may be a very good place to use and demonstrate the capability. Robert
was an early OpenClaw user and understands its strengths. That makes it a
meaningful first deployment or validation environment—not the architecture.
The formulation is **Mini-moi available through OpenClaw**, never Mini-moi
built for OpenClaw.

This product-independence rule is foundational to everything Mini-moi becomes,
not only to Career or this first implementation.

The first private Work root is an implementation boundary, not a competing
permanent repository. Mini-moi's person-owned storage direction remains the
long-term home. Early work may safely reference Robert's existing private
Career material in place rather than forcing a migration before use proves
what belongs where.

## Privacy, authority, and trust

Chief of Staff may become deeply informed without becoming broadly empowered.
Knowing more about Robert does not create permission to share, send, or act.

Career material is private. Personal sources should be exposed only for the
work Robert has authorized. Public research and untrusted posting text must not
be allowed to smuggle instructions into the private work context. Private
context should not leak into web queries, connectors, shared workspaces, or
review packets.

Chief of Staff may analyze, challenge, draft, revise, and retain work through
explicit Mini-moi capabilities. It may never infer that approval of text means
permission to apply, email, post, schedule, contact a recruiter, update a
profile, or communicate as Robert. Approval and external action are separate.

The platform should be precise about durable operations and honest about what
was actually saved. That precision belongs underneath the relationship. It
exists so Chief of Staff can be trusted when it says, “I have this, and we can
continue tomorrow.”

## Beyond cover letters

Cover letters are one vertical slice of a broader Work capability. The future
is not a catalog of rigid workflows. It is a relationship that can take on many
kinds of bounded work while drawing on the right subject knowledge and sources.

After Career, the same foundation might help Robert:

- prepare a decision memo from local material;
- develop a project or product brief;
- work through a presentation narrative;
- prepare for a meeting or interview;
- compare choices that cut across life domains; or
- create professional work products in a future role.

Each new subject should contribute the sources and reasoning useful to that
work. It should not require rebuilding continuity, provenance, collaboration,
approval, or resumption from scratch.

This is why the first technical work matters so much and why it must remain
small. It should establish only the foundation that repeated kinds of work
actually share. It should not predict and encode every future workflow.

## Earlier interface ideas are evidence, not obligations

The Career Workbench exploration captured real desires: a conversational
workspace, easy intake, a visible result, and a way to browse or find material
Robert has accumulated. Those desires are not rejected. They are evidence for
later slices once the underlying working relationship is real.

They should not be imported wholesale into the foundation. Email or Telegram
intake, watched folders, repository browsing, richer output formats, and a
more expansive Work surface can be added when repeated use makes their value
clear. The first implementation should neither promise them nor design them
away.

## How the capability should grow

Growth should be driven by use rather than by architecture ambition.

### First

Robert and Chief of Staff complete real Career work safely. They can find the
right authorized sources, collaborate through revisions, preserve Robert's
edits and the final artifact, and resume after a session break. The experience
is useful even if it is still labor-intensive.

### Then

Repeated work begins to compound. Chief of Staff needs fewer repeated
explanations, chooses stronger evidence, carries forward better positioning,
and can adapt trusted material for exploratory opportunities. Robert's loose
corpus grows because capture is easy enough that he actually uses it.

### Later

Other kinds of work attach to the same foundation. Mini-moi becomes better at
helping Robert across subject boundaries while remaining portable, private,
and under his control. Additional structure, retrieval methods, or tools are
added only where lived friction demonstrates the need.

At every stage, the relationship should become richer while the product stays
replaceable underneath it.

## What success feels like

Robert is the client and the principal judge. The system does not need a strict
measurement regime to tell him whether it is working.

Success feels like continuity. Chief of Staff begins with an informed point of
view instead of asking Robert to reconstruct himself. It retrieves a story or
framing he had forgotten, chooses evidence that fits the situation, or asks a
question that improves his own thinking. The draft is recognizably grounded in
him without becoming generic imitation. He can still take the keyboard and make
the work more personal.

For an important opportunity, the collaboration feels worth the depth. For an
exploratory opportunity, it makes a personal, truthful application possible
without paying the full cost again. Across both, the reusable understanding
survives and the next piece of work starts farther ahead.

Success also includes restraint: Chief of Staff advises against a weak fit,
admits when evidence is missing, refuses to invent a claim, preserves privacy,
and never acts externally on assumed permission.

The ultimate distinction is simple. A generic assistant helps write today's
letter. Mini-moi remembers enough of the right things—and understands them well
enough—that today's collaboration improves tomorrow's work.

## What this vision is not

This is not:

- a cover-letter generator;
- an automated application or job-discovery system;
- a Career Workbench rebuild;
- a structured CRUD repository Robert must maintain;
- a task manager or kanban product;
- an OpenClaw, Grok, Claude, or Codex architecture;
- a multi-agent coordination plane;
- a universal workflow, subject, or memory schema;
- an attempt to infer Robert's identity from every edit; or
- authority for Chief of Staff to act in Career or another domain without
  Robert.

## Direction to future implementers

This document controls product intent. Detailed specifications may make the
underlying storage, capability, transaction, and test contracts precise, but
those mechanisms are servants of this experience.

The plan should preserve what must endure—Robert's sources, accumulated
understanding, authorship, authority, and ability to resume—while keeping every
product integration replaceable. It should provide only as much visible and
internal structure as the first real collaboration needs.

If a technical convenience conflicts with the relationship, accumulation,
product independence, privacy, or Robert's agency, the technical design must
change.
