## Abstract

Something of first-order significance happened to the Northern Hemisphere biosphere between roughly 12,900 and 11,700 years before present. A warming trend reversed sharply, a distinctive geochemical horizon was laid down across multiple continents, the large-mammal fauna of the Americas collapsed, and within a few thousand years agriculture appeared independently in widely separated regions after roughly 300,000 years of anatomically modern humans never having practised it. These facts are not seriously disputed. What they add up to is.

This paper argues that the debate has been badly served by bundling. "The Younger Dryas Impact Hypothesis," "megafaunal overkill," and "the agricultural revolution" are each treated as single propositions when each is a stack of claims of sharply differing evidential strength. Decomposing them changes the answers.

Our directional findings after verification: (1) the impact hypothesis is weakly supported at the level that matters, primarily because diagnostic shock metamorphism is close to absent, and the platinum anomaly is weaker as proponent evidence than v0.1 stated, since its discoverer co-authored the leading refutation; (2) the classic *blitzkrieg* timing model for megafaunal extinction has been falsified by confirmed pre-Clovis human presence, but the broader human-causation case is considerably stronger than v0.1 represented — the most-cited recent synthesis concludes that climate had little major influence, resting principally on the extinctions' unique body-size bias; (3) forage collapse is documented, but its status as *primary driver* is genuinely contested rather than established, and v0.1 overstated it; (4) the proximate trigger of the climate reversal is largely orthogonal to its downstream consequences, so the impact question, however resolved, does not carry the explanatory weight assigned to it; (5) the near-simultaneous independent emergence of agriculture is a genuine phase transition requiring global forcing, and evidence for human population decline at 12.9 ka is stronger than v0.1 allowed, shifting the balance modestly toward compulsion; and (6) complex, dense, monument-building societies demonstrably arose without cereal agriculture, though the cleanest examples require more care than v0.1 exercised.

We flag explicitly where the drafting agent was wrong and was corrected, since the value of a briefing lies partly in its audit trail.

## 2. The central analytical move: decompose the bundle

The most useful step in this area is refusing to argue about named hypotheses and instead arguing about their component claims. Each bundle is a ladder, and each rung is independently evaluable. Proponents habitually defend a high rung by citing evidence for a low one; critics habitually refute a high rung and declare the ladder demolished.

### 2.1 The Younger Dryas Impact Hypothesis (YDIH)

| Rung | Claim | v0.1 | v0.2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Anomalous materials exist at the YD boundary | Moderate-High | **Moderate-High** (unchanged) |
| 2 | Those materials are extraterrestrial in origin | Moderate | **Moderate-Low** ↓ (see §4.1) |
| 3 | The event caused the Younger Dryas cooling | Moderate-High against | **Moderate-High against** (unchanged) |
| 4 | It caused megafaunal extinction and Clovis decline | High against | **Moderate-High against** ↑ (see §6.4) |

Notably, the hypothesis's own defenders concede this structure — Powell and colleagues insist on distinguishing direct chemical and physical evidence of a cosmic event from its proposed secondary effects, observing that if no impact occurred, no relationship to those effects is possible. That framing is correct and cuts against them as often as for them.

### 2.2 Megafaunal extinction — substantially revised

| Rung | Claim | v0.1 | v0.2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Large mammals went extinct rapidly in the Americas near 12.8 ka | High | **High** (unchanged) |
| 2 | Arctic forage base collapsed on a comparable schedule | High | **High** (unchanged) |
| 3 | Habitat change was the *primary* driver | Moderate-High | **Contested — no confident assignment** ↓↓ |
| 4 | Human predation was a contributing factor | Moderate | **Moderate-High** ↑ |

| 6 | Classic *blitzkrieg* timing (extinction follows swiftly on arrival) | High against | **High against** (unchanged) |

Rung 6 has been split out from rung 5 in this version, because the verification pass revealed they were being conflated. *Blitzkrieg* is dead. Human causation is not. That distinction is the core correction of v0.2.

## 3. Why the impact question matters less than it appears

**This remains the paper's most consequential structural claim, and verification strengthened it.**

The impact question is a question about a *proximate trigger*. Whatever caused the Younger Dryas, the Younger Dryas happened. Its downstream consequences — forage change, faunal stress, subsistence disruption — depend on the climate reversal, not on its cause. Establishing an impact would add a chapter; refuting one removes nothing from the sections that follow.

Multiple joint causes remain entirely possible and are excluded by nothing below.

## 4. The impact question, assessed

### 4.1 The strongest case for — with a significant correction

**The Greenland platinum anomaly** was identified by Petaev and colleagues at the YD onset (*PNAS*, 2013). Platinum-group elements are substantially more abundant in meteoritic than terrestrial upper-crustal material, and the ice-core depositional environment and chronology are both excellent.

> **CORRECTION FROM v0.1.** Michail I. Petaev is a listed co-author of Holliday et al. (2023), the comprehensive refutation. The scientist who identified the platinum anomaly signed the document arguing it does not support an impact. Version 0.1 presented the Pt anomaly as the proponents' strongest independent datum without noting this. It substantially weakens the anomaly's evidential role: its discoverer does not read it as proponents do. **[High confidence in the fact; the interpretive weight is a judgement call.]**

**Chronological synchrony.** Bayesian modelling of 354 dates from 23 stratigraphic sections in 12 countries across four continents yields a boundary age of 12,835–12,735 cal BP at 95% probability (Kennett et al., *PNAS*, 2015). **[Moderate — sampling and dating assumptions are contested.]**

**"No crater" is not a refutation.** An airburst leaves none; Tunguska flattened roughly 2,000 km² without one. Critics leading with the missing crater attack a claim proponents do not make. Note, however, that Holliday et al. do make the point, so it remains in play as a component objection. **[High]**

### 4.2 The strongest case against

**The 2023 refutation is not a fringe document.** Holliday et al., *Earth-Science Reviews* 247:104502 (December 2023; 75 pages), carries thirteen authors including Christian Koeberl (Vienna), Philippe Claeys (VUB Brussels), Andrew C. Scott (Royal Holloway), Jeffrey Severinghaus (Scripps), and Mark Boslough (New Mexico), whose specialty is airburst modelling and whose work on the paper was supported by NASA's Solar System Exploration programme. Their structural objection is as damaging as their empirical ones: the YDIH has not converged into a self-consistent scenario spanning orbital dynamics, impact physics, geology, geochemistry, paleobotany, paleoclimatology, and anthropology. **[High]**

**Shocked quartz is nearly absent, and this remains decisive.** Planar deformation features in quartz are the diagnostic impact marker and are ubiquitous at the K-Pg boundary. At the YD boundary, Mahaney reported PDFs in the Venezuelan Andes and then retracted in a companion paper; van Hoesel searched multiple boundary layers across Europe and North America and recovered a single diagnostic grain. For a purportedly continental-scale event this is close to fatal. **[High]** — *This objection originated with the human interlocutor and survives verification intact.*

**The replication dispute favours critics on structure.** Daulton isolated carbonaceous spherules from Arlington Canyon and found no diamond nanocrystals. Proponents respond the samples were not from the YDB layer. "The failures sampled wrong" is unfalsifiable-shaped unless proponents specify the layer in advance and permit a hostile laboratory to sample it. Eighteen years on, this has not happened. **[High — methodological, and the more damaging kind.]**

**Sufficient alternative mechanism.** Glacial meltwater discharge disrupting North Atlantic overturning accounts for the cooling, its duration, and its geographic expression. **[High]**

### 4.3 The exchange has continued past the 2023 paper

For anyone following this thread, the sequence is: Holliday et al. (2023) → Sweatman, Powell & West comment → Holliday et al. rebuttal, with supplementary material deposited at Zenodo (record 13924289, October 2024) because journal space precluded line-by-line response → Powell et al., "Rejection of Holliday et al.'s alleged refutation" (*Earth-Science Reviews*, 2024). The rebuttal's stated themes are the conduct of science and recurrent misapprehensions by proponents, which indicates how far the exchange has moved from evidence toward method.

### 4.4 Assessment

On the full package, critics win decisively. On the narrow claim that an unexplained horizon exists and merits work, proponents retain a legitimate grievance about premature dismissal.

**Weighting note.** Sweatman's review (*Earth-Science Reviews*, 2021) declaring an "overwhelming consensus" is advocacy, not neutral survey. He is a chemical engineer at Edinburgh rather than a geologist, and his separate Göbekli Tepe pillar-astronomy work is considerably weaker.

**The Bretz precedent.** J Harlen Bretz proposed catastrophic flooding for the Channeled Scablands in the 1920s, was ridiculed for four decades, and was vindicated, receiving the Penrose Medal at 96. A genuine case of premature rejection, and the strongest structural argument available to proponents. It establishes the field *can* be wrong in this direction; not that it is wrong here.

## 5. The megafaunal extinctions — major revision

> **PRINCIPAL CORRECTION OF v0.2.** Version 0.1 concluded that habitat/forage collapse was the primary driver and that the field had moved that way. The most-cited recent synthesis argues the reverse. This section is rewritten.

### 5.1 The human-causation case is stronger than v0.1 represented

Svenning et al. (2024), *Cambridge Prisms: Extinction* 2:e5 — a major review with well over a hundred citations — concludes that there is little support for any major influence of climate, either in global extinction patterns or in fine-scale spatiotemporal and mechanistic evidence, and strong and increasing support for human pressures as the key driver, with emerging evidence for onset linked to pre-*Homo sapiens* hominins.

**Their central argument is the body-size bias, and it is strong.** Only 11 of 57 megaherbivore species with body mass ≥1,000 kg survived to the present. This extinction event is unique across the entire Cenozoic in its size selectivity. Earlier Quaternary climate oscillations — of comparable or greater amplitude — did not produce large selective extinctions. And the losses hit as hard in climatically stable regions as in unstable ones, across all biomes from Arctic to tropics.

That pattern is difficult for any habitat-driven account. Climate change should sort species by habitat requirement and physiological tolerance. It should not sort them by body mass across every biome simultaneously. Predation by an efficient hunter *does* sort by body mass, and archaeological traps built for very large animals plus isotopic and protein-residue evidence from spear points confirm that early modern humans targeted the largest mammals.

Supporting work in the same programme: Lemoine, Buitenwerf & Svenning (2023) integrated data on 158 extinct and 329 extant large mammals against 120,000 years of climate reconstruction and found support for *Homo sapiens* causation with little to no climate influence; Bergman et al. (2023) in *Nature Communications* found worldwide Late Pleistocene and Early Holocene population declines in *extant* megafauna associated with human expansion rather than climate — a clever design, since it examines survivors rather than casualties.

**[This is a serious body of work and v0.1 did not engage it. Moderate-High for the size-bias argument specifically.]**

### 5.2 The forage-collapse evidence stands, but its interpretation does not

The underlying data remain excellent. Willerslev et al. (2014), *Nature* 506(7486):47–51, conducted the first large-scale ancient DNA metabarcoding study of circumpolar plant diversity across 50,000 years, with a strikingly international author list (Copenhagen, Tartu, Grenoble, Tromsø, Southampton, Oslo, Moscow, Yakutsk, Wollongong, AMNH). Arctic vegetation consisted of dry steppe-tundra dominated by forbs — non-graminoid herbaceous vascular plants. Diversity declined markedly during the LGM but forbs remained dominant. After 10 kyr BP, moist tundra dominated by woody plants and graminoids appeared.

> **CORRECTION FROM v0.1.** Two overstatements:
>
> 1. v0.1 said forbs were "a large fraction of actual diet." The paper's own conclusion is more hedged: *both* graminoids *and* forbs featured in megafaunal diets. Their stated claim is that this questions the predominance of a graminoid-dominated mammoth steppe — a claim about vegetation composition, not a demonstration that forb loss starved the megafauna.
> 2. v0.1 cited "18 preserved gut-content and scat samples," drawn from press coverage. The paper's figure identifies **eight** ancient megafauna gut and coprolite samples (labelled A–H: mammoths from Drevniy Creek, Lyuba, Mongochen and Finish Creek Valley; a bison; Kolyma and Churapcha rhinos; a Last Chance Creek horse). Use eight.

The quantitative follow-up holds: forbs and graminoids comprised 67–78% of detected taxa before 15 ka, shifting toward shrubs and trees at 22–33% (*Nature Communications*, 2025). And Wang et al. (2021), *Nature*, characterise the mammoth steppe as a regionally complex cryo-arid steppe of forbs, graminoids and willow shrubs — a more textured picture than either the old grassland model or a simple forb model.

**Net:** the vegetation transformation is real and well dated. Whether it *drove* the extinctions is a separate claim, and Svenning et al.'s size-bias and biome-independence arguments cut against it.

### 5.3 The kill-site problem, corrected and still standing

The relevant primary citation is Grayson & Meltzer, "A requiem for North American overkill," *Journal of Archaeological Science* 30(5):585–593 (2003), with their 2015 follow-up revisiting Paleoindian exploitation of extinct mammals. The rebuttal is Fiedel & Haynes, "A premature burial," *JAS* 31:121–131 (2004).

The argument stands: credible kill-site evidence in North America is confined to a small number of genera across a small number of sites, which is thin for a continent-wide extermination.

**But note the structural point v0.1 missed.** Svenning et al.'s case does not depend on kill sites at all. It is a pattern argument — size bias, biome independence, stability independence. A thin kill-site record is a problem for *blitzkrieg*; it is not a refutation of human causation operating over millennia at low intensity on slow-reproducing populations. This is why rungs 5 and 6 had to be separated in §2.2.

### 5.4 Classic blitzkrieg remains falsified

White Sands has survived sustained attack. Bennett et al. (2021), *Science*, dated *Ruppia cirrhosa* seeds to 23,000–21,000 BP. Critics objected reasonably that aquatic plants can take up old dissolved carbon (Madsen et al., comment in *Science* 375; Oviatt et al., *Quaternary Research* 111:138–147; Haynes, *PaleoAmerica* 8:95–98). Pigati et al. (2023), *Science* (doi 10.1126/science.adh5007), applied radiocarbon and luminescence independently and reproduced the range. Holliday et al. (2025), *Science Advances*, dated ancient mud through an independent laboratory at 22,400–20,700 BP. Multiple track layers exist.

Note the irony worth recording: Vance Holliday, lead author of the YDIH refutation, is also lead author of the paper confirming pre-LGM human presence. He is not a man disposed to accept extraordinary claims, and he accepts this one. **[High]**

### 5.5 What does not discriminate

**Island refugia.** v0.1 advanced Wrangel Island mammoths and St. Paul as evidence against climate causation. This was an error. Refugia are consistent with both models — the islands retained habitat *and* lacked humans. Withdrawn, and the withdrawal stands.

### 5.6 Current honest position

This is a genuinely live dispute with credible major syntheses on both sides, and v0.2 declines to assign a confident primary cause. What can be said:

- *Blitzkrieg* timing is dead. **[High]**
- Arctic vegetation was transformed on a comparable schedule. **[High]**
- The size bias is real and is hard for climate accounts. **[High on the fact; Moderate-High on its interpretive force]**
- Humans contributed. **[Moderate-High]**
- Which factor was primary is unresolved, and anyone claiming otherwise is picking a side in an active fight. **[High]**

## 6. The tropical asymmetry, and the resolution of open thread 1

### 6.1 The Younger Dryas was not globally uniform

The event began at high northern latitudes and propagated southward through both atmospheric and oceanic processes, ultimately reaching Antarctica before reversing course (Cheng et al., *PNAS*, 2020). Outside regions directly chilled by the North Atlantic, temperature and moisture responses **decouple**: the tropical hydroclimate response is governed by the southward ITCZ shift, producing drier conditions north of the equator and wetter conditions in the Southern Hemisphere tropics, with soil-moisture change taking up to two centuries to complete (Renssen et al., *Quaternary Science Reviews*, 2018).

Sharpening details:

- Sea surface temperatures off northeastern Brazil **warmed** by 0.25–1.2°C while Greenland crashed; a 3.3°C cooling is recorded north of Venezuela, attributed to ITCZ-driven trade-wind intensification and upwelling.
- Peruvian glaciers **receded** during the YD despite wetter conditions.
- Stríkis et al. (2018) document enhanced precipitation in eastern South America from southward ITCZ displacement and an intensified South American Summer Monsoon.
- Partin et al. (2015), *Nature Communications* 6:8061, title the tropical signal *gradual* onset and recovery.

**[High]**

### 6.2 OPEN THREAD 1 — RESOLVED

v0.1 flagged as its sharpest tension: if tropical South America escaped the cooling, what killed *Megatherium* and the gomphotheres on schedule?

**Answer: it was a different climate signal — Southern Hemisphere warming, not Northern Hemisphere cooling.**

Metcalf et al. (2016), *Science Advances* (doi 10.1126/sciadv.1501682), titled "Synergistic roles of climate warming and human occupation in Patagonian megafaunal extinctions during the Last Deglaciation," identify a narrow megafaunal extinction phase at **12,280 ± 110 years ago**, some 1 to 3 thousand years after initial human presence in the area. The Southern Hemisphere was warming while the North Atlantic froze — the bipolar seesaw — and the extinction pulse tracks that warming.

This is elegant: the *mechanism* is hemisphere-specific while the *structure* is identical. Rapid climate change plus established human presence, with neither sufficient alone. Barnosky & Lindsey (2010), *Quaternary International* 217:10–29, reached the same conclusion continent-wide from 138 megafaunal and 402 archaeological radiocarbon dates: in Patagonia, the Argentine and Uruguayan Pampas, and Brazil, extinctions cluster after human arrival and during intensified climatic change between 11.2 and 13.5 ka, suggesting synergy.

The tension in v0.1 dissolves. The YD did not need to reach South America as cooling; deglacial warming did the work there. **[Moderate-High]**

**Caveat worth carrying.** Barnosky & Lindsey are explicit that the chronology is thin — no taxon other than *Mylodon* has more than seven robust dates, and many have fewer than three. South America lost roughly 50–53 genera, about 82–83% of mammals over 44 kg, the most severe continental loss anywhere. Conclusions rest on sparse data.

**New and relevant:** Prates & Perez and colleagues, *Science Advances* (2025, doi 10.1126/sciadv.adx2615), report that extinct megafauna dominated human subsistence in southern South America before 11,600 years ago. This is direct evidence of heavy human exploitation and cuts against the thin-kill-site argument as applied to South America specifically. It should be read before v0.3.

## 7. Agriculture as inflection

### 7.1 The scale argument is sound

Anatomically modern humans span roughly 300,000 years with no agriculture anywhere. Then, within a few millennia, it appears independently in unconnected regions on separate continents. This is a phase transition, not gradual accumulation. Diffusion cannot explain independent invention in the Levant, China, Mesoamerica, New Guinea, and — as recent work establishes — southwestern Amazonia. **[High]**

### 7.2 Push versus pull

**Push (compulsion).** The subsistence base failed, forcing cultivation. Bar-Yosef and Hillman argued the Younger Dryas drove Natufian populations toward deliberate planting as cooling collapsed wild stands. Abu Hureyra, Syria is the type site — and, notably, also a YDIH site, a coincidence flagged for scrutiny in §10.

**Pull (release of constraint).** Richerson, Boyd & Bettinger (2001), *American Antiquity* 66(3):387–411, argued agriculture was *impossible* during the Pleistocene — too climatically variable, atmospheric CO₂ too low — and became *compulsory* in the stable Holocene. Climate explanation without catastrophe.

**Discriminating predictions.** Push predicts hardship, discontinuity, abandonment. Pull predicts uneven, optional adoption with foragers persisting where they did well.

**Assessment.** Occupation at Abu Hureyra is continuous through the YD — the same community shifted from harvesting wild stands to cultivation in place. Foragers persisted alongside farmers for millennia and frequently declined to adopt; Australia never did; the Pacific Northwest never did. **[Moderate-High for pull as primary, with push operating regionally — but see §7.4.]**

### 7.3 Correction retained: not simultaneous

Agriculture appears in roughly eleven independent centres over several thousand years. The early-Holocene clustering is real; strict simultaneity is not supported. The traditional count of four early-Holocene centres (China, Near East, southwestern Mexico, northwestern South America) has been expanded — Lombardo et al. (2020) make the case for southwestern Amazonia as a fifth. **[High]**

### 7.4 OPEN THREAD 2 — CORRECTED, NOT RESOLVED

> **CORRECTION FROM v0.1.** Section 6.4 of v0.1 stated at Moderate confidence that no human population crash is visible at 12.9 ka. **This was wrong, or at minimum badly overconfident.**

Anderson, Goodyear, Kennett & West (2011), *Quaternary International*, tested this directly with frequency analyses of Paleoindian projectile points from the Paleoindian Database of the Americas, time series of lithic assemblages from eleven southeastern US quarry sites, and summed-probability analyses of radiocarbon dates. All three suggest significant decline or reorganisation at 12.9 ka, continuing through the early YD centuries and rebounding by its end. Heavily used stone quarries appear largely abandoned; diagnostic point counts and cultural radiocarbon dates decline substantially over large areas.

Independently, Y-chromosome work on Haplogroup Q (*PLOS ONE*, 2022, doi 10.1371/journal.pone.0271971) supports South American settlement before 18,000 years ago and argues the YD may have caused substantial loss of Native American lineages, potentially explaining the low present-day frequency of Q-Z780.

**Caveats that keep this open rather than closed:**
- Kennett and West are YDIH proponents; the 2011 paper is not a neutral test.
- Summed-probability radiocarbon analysis has known taphonomic and sampling biases.
- Population *reorganisation* is not population *collapse*, and the authors say "decline and/or reorganization."
- Lineage loss is not the same as effective population size reduction.

**Revised assessment: there is real evidence of demographic disruption at the YD onset in North America, with recovery by the YD's end. This shifts rung 4 of §2.3 upward and gives the push model more support than v0.1 allowed. [Moderate]**

### 7.5 Expansion mechanics

Demic diffusion at roughly one kilometre per year is well documented and repeatedly confirmed by ancient DNA: Anatolian farmer ancestry sweeping across Europe and largely replacing rather than converting Mesolithic foragers; the Bantu expansion; the Austronesian expansion. Agriculture wins through higher fertility, more calories per hectare, and a surplus supporting specialists including warriors. **[High]**

## 8. Complex society does not require cereal agriculture

### 8.1 Counterexamples, with corrections

> **CORRECTION FROM v0.1.** Two examples were stated too cleanly.

**Göbekli Tepe.** v0.1 described it as built by non-farmers. The current picture from the Taş Tepeler research programme — Göbekli Tepe, Karahan Tepe, Sayburç, Çakmaktepe, Sefertepe, excavated largely under the German Archaeological Institute with Lee Clare and colleagues — is a **mixed economy**: communities hunting gazelle and wild cattle while processing wild cereals including wheat and barley, and edging toward permanent settlement. Wheat grains and stone-oven remains at Karahan Tepe raise the possibility of early cultivation and bread production.

The defensible claim is therefore narrower but still decisive: **monumental architecture preceded full agricultural sedentism.** Occupation at Göbekli Tepe ceased around 8200 BC with no evidence that its people ever adopted domesticated grains or herding. Coordinated monumental labour did not wait for farming. **[High for the narrower claim]**

**Casarabe (Llanos de Mojos).** v0.1 implied this was complexity without agriculture. It was not — the Casarabe were pre-Hispanic agriculturalists who transformed seasonally flooded savannah into agricultural and aquacultural landscapes, and they grew maize. What Prümers et al. (2022), *Nature*, actually establish is **low-density tropical urbanism** in Amazonia: two sites of 147 ha and 315 ha in a four-tiered settlement system across roughly 4,500 km², with stepped platforms, conical pyramids up to 22 m, ranked concentric polygonal banks, causeways, canals and reservoirs, dated AD 500–1400. That refutes "no fields means small populations," not "civilisation requires agriculture." Use it for the former.

**Examples that survive unmodified:**
- **Pacific Northwest Coast** — sedentary, dense, materially rich, hereditary rank, slavery, formal property in resource sites, elaborate art, no agriculture.
- **Jōmon Japan** — sedentary villages, among the world's earliest pottery, millennia on foraging.
- **Poverty Point, Louisiana** — massive earthworks by foragers.

### 8.2 The health economics favoured foraging

Skeletal evidence across the transition is consistent: early farmers shorter, more dental caries and enamel hypoplasia, more anemia, more infectious disease from density and livestock proximity, higher mortality. Agriculture purchased population and storable surplus and paid in individual health and exposure to drought, monocrop failure, and raiding. **[High]**

### 8.3 The real variable is storable, concentrated, seizable surplus

Tillage is one route, not the definition. Northwest Coast societies developed rank and slavery on predictable seasonal salmon bonanzas. This reframing explains the counterexamples, which "agriculture equals civilisation" cannot.

Scott's harder version: early states cultivated *grain specifically* because grain ripens on a schedule and can be assessed, seized, and stored; tubers resist taxation. Cereal agriculture was not selected because it fed people better but because it fed states. **[Moderate-High as framework; contested in detail.]**

### 8.4 Amazonia: the case that breaks the binary — now with a chronology

Levis et al. (2017), *Science* 355(6328):925–931, overlaid known archaeological sites against distributions of 85 woody species domesticated by pre-Columbian peoples across 1,104 forest plots. **Domesticated species are five times more likely than non-domesticated species to be hyperdominant**, and their relative abundance and richness rise on and around archaeological sites; in southwestern and eastern Amazonia, distance to archaeological sites strongly predicts both.

**The published critique should be carried alongside it.** McMichael and Bush comment in *Science* (doi 10.1126/science.aan8347) that the study downplays five centuries of post-Columbian influence, which they argue likely played a larger role in the observed floristic patterns. A separate critique notes many cultivated fruit trees are early-succession species or poor competitors — peach palm stops fruiting as forest overshadows it — so their distribution may reflect disturbance history rather than deliberate propagation. **These are serious objections and v0.1 omitted them.**

**The chronology, which v0.1 lacked, is striking.** Lombardo et al. (2020), *Nature* 581(7807):190–193, document in the Llanos de Moxos: manioc at ~10,350 cal BP, squash at ~10,250 cal BP, maize at ~6,850 cal BP — and, from **~10,850 cal BP**, the construction of a landscape ultimately comprising roughly **4,700 artificial forest islands** within treeless seasonally flooded savannah.

That start date sits within roughly a millennium of the YD termination (~11,700 BP). Amazonian landscape modification is not a late development. It begins essentially as soon as the Holocene does — which strengthens §7.1's phase-transition argument and makes southwestern Amazonia a fifth independent early-Holocene domestication centre. **[High — this is the most valuable single addition in v0.2.]**

**Terra preta** — anthropogenic dark earths of charcoal, ceramic sherds, bone and organic waste — remain measurably more fertile than surrounding soils centuries later. Chronology remains an open thread (§10).

### 8.5 The anti-tax subsistence base

A managed forest is dispersed, does not ripen on schedule, and produces no granary to seize — close to the ideal illegible subsistence base in Scott's sense. Amazonian societies achieved dense populations and monumental construction without generating the concentrated, countable surplus permitting external extraction. A third path, not a way station.

### 8.6 Humans as replacement megafauna

Janzen & Martin (1982), *Science* 215:19–27, "Neotropical anachronisms: the fruits the gomphotheres ate," holds that Amazonian trees with large fleshy fruits co-evolved with gomphotheres and ground sloths, and were stranded by their loss. People assumed the role — the clustering of *castanhais* on archaeological sites and the hyperdominance of useful species represent humans occupying a vacated ecological function, selecting for what they wished to eat. Galetti et al. (2018), *Biological Reviews* 93:845–862, treat the broader ecological legacy. **[Moderate-High]**

### 11.13 A note on international coverage

The literature here is more international than a US-centric reading suggests, and it is worth noting where the work is actually done. The Arctic vegetation programme is Danish-Estonian-French-Norwegian-Russian (Copenhagen, Tartu, Grenoble, Tromsø, Yakutsk). The Amazonian archaeology is Brazilian-Bolivian-German-British-Swiss (INPA, USP, UMSA La Paz, DAI Bonn, Exeter, Bern). The Neolithic Anatolian work is German-Turkish (DAI, Şanlıurfa). The megafauna synthesis camp is Danish (Aarhus/ECONOVO); the leading climate-side paper is German (Max Planck Jena). The YDIH refutation is US-Austrian-Belgian-British. South American extinction chronology is Chilean-Argentine-US.

Two practical consequences: much relevant work appears in Spanish and Portuguese in regional venues (*Relaciones de la Sociedad Argentina de Antropología*, *Latin American Antiquity*, ABEQUA proceedings, *Zeitschrift für Archäologie Außereuropäischer Kulturen*) and is not indexed alongside the English literature. And the Russian-language mammoth-steppe tradition — Yurtsev, Sher, Tikhonov, Savvinov — predates and informs the aDNA work but is under-cited in Western syntheses. Both are worth a dedicated pass in a later version.
