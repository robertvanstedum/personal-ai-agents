## Abstract

Something of first-order significance happened to the Northern Hemisphere biosphere between roughly 12,900 and 11,700 years before present. A warming trend reversed sharply, a distinctive geochemical horizon was laid down across multiple continents, the large-mammal fauna of the Americas collapsed, and within a few thousand years agriculture appeared independently in widely separated regions after roughly 300,000 years of anatomically modern humans never having practised it. These facts are not seriously disputed. What they add up to is.

This paper argues that the debate has been badly served by bundling. "The Younger Dryas Impact Hypothesis," "megafaunal overkill," and "the agricultural revolution" are each treated as single propositions when each is a stack of claims of sharply differing evidential strength. Decomposing them changes the answers.

Our directional findings: (1) the impact hypothesis is weakly supported at the level that matters, primarily because diagnostic shock metamorphism is close to absent, but the anomalous boundary horizon is real and merits continued work; (2) the classic human-overkill model for megafaunal extinction has been substantially falsified by confirmed pre-Clovis human presence and by a thin kill-site record, while the forage-collapse account is now positively supported by direct evidence from ancient plant DNA and preserved gut contents; (3) the proximate trigger of the climate reversal is largely orthogonal to its downstream consequences, so the impact question, however resolved, does not carry the explanatory weight assigned to it; (4) the near-simultaneous, independent emergence of agriculture is a genuine phase transition requiring a global forcing, though the evidence favours release-of-constraint over catastrophe-driven compulsion; and (5) complex, dense, monument-building societies demonstrably arose without cereal agriculture, which means the causal variable is storable concentrated surplus rather than tillage as such.

We flag explicitly where the drafting agent's initial position was wrong and was moved by argument, since the value of a briefing lies partly in its audit trail.

## 1. Provenance and method

This document originated in an unrelated exchange about a high-school algebra worksheet, moved through a critique of mathematics pedagogy, and arrived at the terminal Pleistocene by way of Randall Carlson's sacred geometry courses. That path is not incidental. The organising complaint throughout — that technique is taught without motivation, and that "contested" is a word used to avoid argument — shaped how this paper is structured.

The method was adversarial. Claims were advanced, attacked, conceded, and revised. Positions changed in both directions. Where the drafting agent hedged, it was called on it; where the human's position over-reached, it was pushed back. Section 8 records the movement, because a briefing that hides its own error rate is less useful than one that shows it.

## 2. The central analytical move: decompose the bundle

The single most useful step in this whole area is refusing to argue about named hypotheses and instead arguing about their component claims. Each bundle below is a ladder, and each rung is independently evaluable. Proponents habitually defend a high rung by citing evidence for a low one; critics habitually refute a high rung and declare the ladder demolished.

### 2.1 The Younger Dryas Impact Hypothesis (YDIH)

| Rung | Claim | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Anomalous materials exist at the YD boundary (Pt, microspherules, meltglass, nanodiamonds) | Reasonably supported **[Moderate-High]** |
| 2 | Those materials are extraterrestrial in origin | Unresolved, leaning against **[Moderate]** |
| 3 | The event caused the Younger Dryas cooling | Weak; a sufficient alternative mechanism exists **[Moderate-High against]** |
| 4 | It caused megafaunal extinction and Clovis decline | Weakest; contradicted by global pattern **[High against]** |

Notably, the hypothesis's own defenders concede this structure — Powell and colleagues insist on distinguishing direct chemical and physical evidence of a cosmic event from its proposed secondary effects, observing that if no impact occurred, no relationship to those effects is possible. That is the correct framing and it cuts against them as often as for them.

### 2.2 Megafaunal extinction

| Rung | Claim | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Large mammals went extinct rapidly in the Americas near 12.8 ka | **[High]** |
| 2 | Their forage base collapsed on the same schedule | **[High]** — see §4.3 |
| 3 | Habitat change was the primary driver | **[Moderate-High]** |
| 4 | Human predation was a contributing factor | **[Moderate]** |
| 5 | Human predation was the primary driver ("overkill") | **[High against]** — see §4.1–4.2 |

### 2.3 The agricultural transition

| Rung | Claim | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Agriculture appears independently in multiple centres within a few millennia after ~300 kyr of absence | **[High]** |
| 2 | This requires a global rather than local explanation | **[High]** |
| 3 | Holocene climate stability plus rising CO₂ removed a binding constraint | **[Moderate-High]** |
| 4 | Forager subsistence collapsed, forcing the transition | **[Low-Moderate]**, regionally variable |
| 5 | Complex society requires agriculture | **[High against]** — see §7 |

## 3. The impact question

### 3.1 The strongest case for

**The Greenland platinum anomaly** is the best single datum. Petaev and colleagues identified a large Pt spike in ice-core records at the YD onset. Platinum-group elements are substantially more abundant in meteoritic than in terrestrial upper-crustal material, the measurement came from a group independent of the original proponents, and the depositional environment and chronology are both excellent. **[High that the anomaly is real; Moderate on its interpretation.]**

**Chronological synchrony.** Bayesian modelling of 354 dates from 23 stratigraphic sections in 12 countries across four continents yields a boundary age of 12,835–12,735 cal BP at 95% probability, overlapping the Greenland Pt peak and the earliest YD onset in six proxy records. A synchronous marker horizon on four continents is not easily produced by ordinary sedimentary processes. **[Moderate — depends on sampling and dating assumptions that critics contest.]**

**"No crater" is not a refutation.** An airburst leaves none; Tunguska flattened approximately 2,000 km² without one. Critics who lead with the missing crater attack a claim proponents do not make. **[High]**

**Claimed replication.** Proponents count direct nanodiamond replication at eight sites, arguing that failed replications sampled the wrong stratigraphic layers. **[Low-Moderate — see §3.2]**

### 3.2 The strongest case against

**The 2023 refutation is not a fringe document.** Holliday et al. carries thirteen authors including Christian Koeberl and Philippe Claeys — impact geochemists with substantial K-Pg credentials — and Mark Boslough, whose specialty is airburst modelling. His position is that the energetics do not scale to hemispheric effects. When the specialists in impact signatures report that the signatures are absent, that is not gatekeeping. **[High]**

**Shocked quartz is nearly absent, and this is the decisive problem.** Planar deformation features in quartz are the diagnostic impact marker and are ubiquitous at the K-Pg boundary. At the YD boundary: Mahaney reported PDFs in the Venezuelan Andes and then retracted in a companion paper, stating that no irrefutable PDFs or shock-melted quartz were detected; van Hoesel searched multiple boundary layers across Europe and North America and recovered a single diagnostic grain. For a purportedly continental-scale event, this is close to fatal. **[High]**

**The replication dispute favours critics on structure, not just substance.** Daulton isolated carbonaceous spherules from Arlington Canyon and found no diamond nanocrystals. Proponents respond that the samples were not from the YDB layer. "The failures sampled wrong" is an unfalsifiable-shaped defence unless proponents specify the layer in advance and permit a hostile laboratory to sample it. Eighteen years on, this has not happened. **[High — this is a methodological, not an empirical, objection, and it is the more damaging kind.]**

**Direct attack on synchrony.** Van Hoesel radiocarbon-dated fourteen individual charcoal particles at Usselo, Netherlands — internally consistent, showing wildfires postdating the Allerød–YD boundary. **[Moderate-High]**

**Sufficient alternative mechanism.** Glacial meltwater discharge disrupting North Atlantic overturning circulation accounts for the cooling, its duration, and its geographic expression. An impact is not required and adds no explanatory value at this rung. **[High]**

### 3.3 Assessment

On the full package, the critics win decisively. On the narrow claim that an unexplained geochemical horizon exists and merits continued investigation, proponents have a legitimate grievance about premature dismissal.

**Weighting note:** Sweatman's review declaring an "overwhelming consensus" that the impact claims should be accepted is advocacy, not neutral survey. He is a chemical engineer rather than a geologist, and his separate work on Göbekli Tepe pillar astronomy is considerably weaker than his YD work. This does not make him wrong; it means the review should not be read as a field survey.

**The Bretz precedent.** J Harlen Bretz proposed catastrophic flooding for the Channeled Scablands in the 1920s, was ridiculed for four decades, and was vindicated, receiving the Penrose Medal at 96. This is a genuine case of premature rejection by a conservative discipline and is the strongest structural argument available to YDIH proponents. It establishes that the field can be wrong in this direction; it does not establish that it is wrong here.

### 3.4 Why this matters less than it appears

**This is the paper's most consequential claim.** The impact question is a question about a *proximate trigger*. Whatever caused the Younger Dryas, the Younger Dryas happened. Its downstream consequences — forage collapse, faunal extinction, subsistence disruption — depend on the climate reversal, not on its cause. Establishing an impact would add a chapter to the story; refuting one removes nothing from the sections that follow.

Multiple joint causes remain entirely possible and are not excluded by anything above.

## 4. The megafaunal extinctions

### 4.1 Classic overkill has been falsified on its own terms

Martin's blitzkrieg model made a specific timing prediction: extinction follows rapidly on human arrival. That prediction has failed.

The White Sands footprints have survived sustained attack. Initial 2021 dates from *Ruppia cirrhosa* seeds gave 23,000–21,000 BP; critics objected, reasonably, that aquatic plants can take up old dissolved carbon and read too old. A 2023 follow-up applied two independent dating approaches and reproduced the range. A 2025 *Science Advances* study dated ancient mud rather than seeds, through an independent laboratory, returning 22,400–20,700 BP. Multiple distinct track layers exist, which is why excavators can offer critics a choice of horizons. **[High]**

Humans in New Mexico at 23 ka and megafaunal extinction at ~12.8 ka gives roughly ten millennia of coexistence. Whatever killed the megafauna, it was not the arrival of people. **[High]**

### 4.2 The kill-site problem

North America lost roughly three dozen genera. Credible kill-site evidence exists for mammoth and mastodon only, across on the order of fifteen sites. For a continent-wide extermination of millions of animals sustained over millennia, the archaeological record is remarkably thin. Grayson and Meltzer have pressed this for years and it remains the strongest single empirical argument against overkill. **[High]**

The commonsense version: if hunting did it, there should be evidence, and there mostly is not.

### 4.3 Forage collapse is positively evidenced, not merely inferred

This is the best-documented element in the entire debate and the one that most changed the drafting agent's assessment.

Willerslev and colleagues sequenced ancient plant DNA from permafrost cores at 17 sites across northern Russia, Canada, and Alaska spanning 50,000 years. The result overturned the textbook picture of the mammoth steppe as grass-dominated. It was dominated by **forbs** — broad-leaved herbaceous flowering plants including yarrow, sagebrush, and northern plantain — which are more nutritious than grasses, plausibly a key protein source, and more digestible. Around 10,000 years ago the forbs were replaced by moist tundra dominated by woody plants, grasses, sedges, and mosses.

Critically, the team closed the loop: 18 preserved gut-content and scat samples from mammoth, woolly rhinoceros, horse, reindeer, and elk showed forbs comprising a large fraction of actual diet. This is not inference from vegetation change. It is documented removal of the specific food. **[High]**

A 2025 *Nature Communications* study using lake sediment DNA from Siberia and Alaska quantifies the shift: forbs and graminoids comprised 67–78% of detected taxa before 15 ka, with shrubs and trees rising to 22–33% afterward. **[High]**

### 4.4 Causal direction: an open question

Zimov's counter-model reverses the arrow — megaherbivores *maintained* the steppe through grazing, trampling, and nutrient cycling, so losing the animals caused the vegetation shift rather than the reverse. This is a serious position with a real mechanism.

Current evidence tilts toward vegetation leading. The 2025 modelling found vegetation turnover *lagged by one time-slice* to be the strongest predictor of plant extinction, ahead of megafaunal extinction and ahead of July temperature. **[Moderate — this is a single modelling result and the feedback is genuinely bidirectional.]**

### 4.5 What does not discriminate

**Island refugia.** The drafting agent initially advanced Wrangel Island mammoths (surviving to ~4,000 BP) and St. Paul Island (~5,600 BP) as evidence against climate causation. This was an error. Refugia are consistent with *both* models: the islands retained steppe-like habitat *and* lacked humans. The datum does not discriminate and was withdrawn.

### 4.6 What remains unexplained

Global asynchrony is the strongest surviving problem for any single-cause climate account. Australian megafauna are lost around 40 ka; Madagascar's and New Zealand's within the last two millennia; Eurasia's staggered. The variable that tracks globally is human arrival, not any single climate event. Additionally, earlier glacial terminations produced comparable climate swings without comparable extinctions. **[High — this is the strongest argument that humans mattered somewhere in the causal chain.]**

**Working synthesis:** long low-density human presence; severe climate reversal stressing already-thinned, slow-reproducing K-selected populations; human predation suppressing recovery. Neither factor alone accounts for the global pattern; together they do. **[Moderate-High]**

## 5. The tropical asymmetry

The Younger Dryas was not a globally uniform event, and this is underweighted in popular treatments.

The event began at high northern latitudes and propagated southward. Outside regions directly chilled by the North Atlantic, temperature and moisture responses **decouple**. The tropical signal is governed by the ITCZ shifting south in response to North Atlantic cooling, producing drier conditions north of the equator and **wetter conditions in the Southern Hemisphere tropics**, with an intensified South American Summer Monsoon.

Two details sharpen this considerably:

- Sea surface temperatures off northeastern Brazil **warmed** by 0.25–1.2°C while Greenland was crashing. Cooling of 3.3°C is recorded north of Venezuela, attributed to ITCZ-driven trade wind intensification and upwelling.
- Peruvian glaciers **receded** during the YD despite wetter conditions, consistent with regional warmth.

The tempo differs as well. Tropical soil moisture change was gradual, taking up to two centuries to complete; one paper's title states that onset and recovery in the tropics were gradual rather than abrupt. **[High]**

### 5.1 Consequence: South America as a natural control

Tropical South America experienced a warmer, wetter, slower-arriving version of the event. This makes it a control case, and it developed along a distinct trajectory — forest management rather than cereal agriculture (§7.3).

### 5.2 The tension this creates

South America lost its megafauna anyway: *Megatherium*, *Toxodon*, *Macrauchenia*, glyptodonts, gomphotheres, on broadly the same schedule as North America. If the continent escaped the temperature collapse, the YD cooling cannot be the operative mechanism there.

This is a genuine problem for climate-primary accounts and the strongest surviving argument for either a human role or region-specific causes. **[Moderate-High]** It is flagged as an open thread (§9).

## 6. Agriculture as inflection

### 6.1 The scale argument is sound

Anatomically modern humans span roughly 300,000 years with no agriculture anywhere. Then, within a few millennia, it appears independently in unconnected regions on separate continents. This is a phase transition, not gradual accumulation of technique. Diffusion cannot explain independent invention in the Levant, China, Mesoamerica, and New Guinea. A global forcing is required. **[High]**

### 6.2 Push versus pull

Two families of explanation fit the timing:

**Push (compulsion).** The old subsistence base failed, forcing cultivation. Bar-Yosef and Hillman argued the Younger Dryas drove Natufian populations toward deliberate planting as abrupt cooling collapsed the wild stands they depended on. Abu Hureyra, Syria is the type site — and, incidentally, one of the YDIH sites.

**Pull (release of constraint).** Richerson, Boyd, and Bettinger argued that agriculture was *impossible* during the Pleistocene — too climatically variable, atmospheric CO₂ too low for cultivation to repay effort — and became *compulsory* in the stable Holocene. This is a climate explanation without catastrophe.

**Discriminating predictions:** push predicts hardship, discontinuity, and site abandonment at the transition. Pull predicts uneven, optional adoption with foragers persisting where they were doing well.

**Assessment.** The record contains more of the second. Occupation at Abu Hureyra is continuous through the YD — the same community shifted from harvesting wild stands to cultivation *in place*, which is stress and adaptation rather than collapse. Foragers persisted alongside farmers for millennia, frequently declining to adopt. Australia never took it up. Pacific Northwest societies never did. **[Moderate-High for pull as primary, with push operating regionally.]**

### 6.3 Correction to the "worldwide simultaneous" framing

Agriculture appears in roughly eleven independent centres spread over several thousand years: Levant ~11,500 BP, China and Mesoamerica ~9,000 BP, New Guinea ~7,000 BP, with Andean and African centres. The early-Holocene clustering is real and demands explanation; strict simultaneity is not supported. **[High]**

### 6.4 A testable proposition not yet checked

Severe human population crashes leave genomic signatures — reduced effective population size, loss of diversity, bottleneck structure. If the YD caused widespread human die-off, it should be visible. Present understanding is that it is not: well-attested bottlenecks sit much earlier, with no comparable global signal at 12.9 ka. Regional turnover (Clovis to Folsom, various local discontinuities) is attested; species-level crisis is not. **[Moderate — this warrants direct verification and is an open thread.]**

### 6.5 Expansion mechanics

Demic diffusion at approximately one kilometre per year is well documented and repeatedly confirmed by ancient DNA: Anatolian farmer ancestry sweeping across Europe and largely replacing rather than converting Mesolithic foragers; the Bantu expansion; the Austronesian expansion. Agriculture does not win by persuasion. It wins through higher fertility (shorter birth intervals), more calories per hectare, and a surplus that supports specialists including warriors. **[High]**

## 7. Complex society does not require cereal agriculture

### 7.1 The counterexamples

- **Göbekli Tepe** — monumental megalithic architecture requiring coordinated labour, built by non-farmers, predating regional domestication.
- **Pacific Northwest Coast** — sedentary, dense, materially rich, with hereditary rank, slavery, formal property in resource sites, and elaborate art. No agriculture; salmon and cedar sufficed.
- **Jōmon Japan** — sedentary villages and among the world's earliest pottery, sustained for millennia on foraging.
- **Poverty Point, Louisiana** — massive earthworks constructed by foragers.

**[High]**

### 7.2 The health economics favoured foraging

Skeletal evidence across the transition is consistent: early farmers were shorter, with more dental caries and enamel hypoplasia, more anemia, more infectious disease from density and livestock proximity, and higher mortality. Foragers had more varied diets and worked fewer hours. Agriculture purchased population and storable surplus and paid in individual health and in exposure to drought, monocrop failure, and raiding. **[High]**

### 7.3 The real variable is storable, concentrated, seizable surplus

Tillage is one route to this, not the definition of it. Northwest Coast societies developed rank and slavery on predictable seasonal salmon bonanzas — concentrated, storable surplus without agriculture. This reframing preserves the counterexamples and explains them, which "agriculture equals civilisation" cannot.

Scott's harder version: early states cultivated *grain specifically* because grain ripens on a schedule and can be assessed, seized, and stored. Tubers resist taxation — they remain in the ground and are dug as needed. On this reading cereal agriculture was not selected because it fed people better but because it fed states. **[Moderate-High as a framework; contested in detail.]**

### 7.4 Amazonia: the case that breaks the binary

Levis and colleagues demonstrated that domesticated species are hyperdominant in Amazonian forests — far more abundant than chance predicts, with abundance rising systematically near archaeological sites. This is a forest whose species composition was edited over centuries.

- **Brazil nut (*Bertholletia excelsa*)** is the cleanest case: groves cluster on former settlement sites, and the species depends on agouti caching plus human transport. Contemporary *castanhais* are substantially artefacts of past planting.
- **Peach palm (*Bactris gasipaes*)** is genuinely domesticated with no straightforward wild progenitor.
- Cacao, açaí, cupuaçu and multiple palms show the same signature.
- **Terra preta** — anthropogenic dark earths built from charcoal, ceramic sherds, bone, and organic waste — remain measurably more fertile than surrounding soils centuries later. This is not accidental and not rapid.

**[High]**

LiDAR has demolished the corollary assumption that no fields meant small populations: the Casarabe sites in the Llanos de Mojos comprise a settlement network with monumental architecture, causeways, and reservoirs, with comparable results arriving from the Upper Xingu and the Andean foothills.

**Correction on framing.** The strong "savanna to jungle" transition — Amazonia fragmenting into forest refugia separated by grassland during glacials — is Haffer's 1969 refugia hypothesis and has fallen substantially out of favour. Paleoecological work suggests forest largely persisted through the LGM, cooler and drier, with savanna expanding at the margins. The directional instinct (a different, drier forest) is correct; the magnitude is not. The cultivation evidence is unaffected. **[Moderate-High]**

### 7.5 The anti-tax subsistence base

A managed forest is dispersed, does not ripen on a schedule, and produces no granary to seize. It is close to the ideal illegible subsistence base in Scott's sense. Amazonian societies achieved dense populations and monumental construction *without* generating the concentrated, countable surplus that permits external extraction. This is a third path, not a way station between foraging and farming.

### 7.6 Humans as replacement megafauna

Janzen and Martin's megafaunal dispersal hypothesis holds that Amazonian trees bearing large fleshy fruits — many oversized for any extant disperser — co-evolved with gomphotheres and ground sloths. Their loss stranded those trees.

People assumed the role. The clustering of *castanhais* on archaeological sites and the hyperdominance of useful species represent humans occupying a vacated ecological function, this time selecting for what they wished to eat. **[Moderate-High]**

This closes a loop: the megafauna disappear; within a few thousand years the same forest is being propagated by a different large mammal.

## 9. Open threads for v0.2

1. **South American extinction chronology.** If the continent escaped the temperature collapse, what killed *Megatherium* and the gomphotheres on schedule? Priority thread — it is the sharpest live tension in the paper.
2. **Human genomic bottleneck at 12.9 ka.** Stated as absent at Moderate confidence (§6.4). Needs direct verification against current aDNA literature.
3. **Causal direction in the steppe collapse.** Zimov's herbivore-maintenance model versus vegetation-leading. Currently resting on one modelling result.
4. **The human's "Part 2," deferred.** Explicitly held for a later session.
5. **Terra preta chronology.** How much predates 5 ka? Bears on whether Amazonian management is an early-Holocene response or a later development.
6. **Lombardo et al. on early southwestern Amazonian cultivation (~10.3 ka).** If robust, this places forest management immediately post-YD and strengthens §7.3 considerably.
7. **Kill-site count.** The "roughly fifteen sites, two genera" figure should be checked against the most recent compilation.
8. **Abu Hureyra double-duty.** The site is both the agriculture type site and a YDIH site. Worth examining whether that coincidence has influenced interpretation in either literature.
